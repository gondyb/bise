why

    
you didn't become an engineer
to babysit robots.

    
yet here you are.

    
today you started five things. you watched output scroll. you answered "should i continue?" eleven times. at 6pm you'd shipped more than ever, and felt like you did nothing.

    
that's not you. every few minutes, something pulled you out. you never got to think.

    
you lost your flow. bise gives it back.

    
you talk, whenever, however. i run the agents in the background, answer the obvious questions myself, and only tap your shoulder when it matters.

what it feels like
ten things you'll notice in the first hour.

    

      
talk whenever. you never wait.
say the next thing while the last one runs. your composer is never locked.

      
talk to any agent, anytime.
⌥ + a number, or @name. ask it why, push it, then go back to main. nobody has to stop.

      
change your mind mid-run.
a correction lands in the running agent. ✓ it got it, ✓✓ it read it.

      
agents sync on their own.
every agent can talk to every other one. they ask, share and hand off quietly, folded out of your way.

      
you're not the router.
main answers the obvious questions itself, the way you would, and tells you why.

      
only the real decisions reach you.
one card, a couple of choices, one key. then back to what you were doing.

      
nothing gets dropped.
when an agent stops half-way, main picks the work back up from where it was.

      
worktrees, only when they help.
agents share your folder. when one really needs isolation, it makes a worktree, and cleans it up after.

      
plug in everything. leave it all on.
GitHub, Linear, Sentry, Slack, your docs, your database: every tool and all your context, always on. you never pick what to enable. agents find the right tool when they need it and call it from code, so a hundred servers don't crowd their heads.

      
bring your plugins.
your skills, plugins and MCP servers come with you. switching costs you an afternoon, not a month.
