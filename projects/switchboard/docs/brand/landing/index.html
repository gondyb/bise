why

a day of coding with agents feels empty.

you started five things. you shipped some of them, maybe. you spent the day watching output scroll, answering "should i continue?", checking which terminal was doing what.

it's not that you did nothing. it's that you never got to think. every few minutes, something pulled you out. you lost control, and got nothing back for it.

that feeling has a name: no flow.

bise is built around one idea: protect your flow. you talk, whenever you want, however it comes out. i run the agents in the background. i answer the obvious questions myself. you only hear about what really needs you.

built for humans piloting hundreds of robots, from a distance.*

how it works

you talk. i run the agents. you stay in flow.

1

say it, and keep going.

type what's on your mind, mid-thought, as often as you want. no prompt engineering, no workflow to design. you never wait for a turn: i'm always listening.

2

i start the agents.

one agent per thing, in the background, in your own folder. they know about each other, and talk to each other quietly, so you don't have to route anything.

3

you only hear what matters.

the obvious questions, i answer myself. real decisions come to you as a card. when something ships, you get a ♡. that's it.

what you get

opinionated, so you don't have to be.

∿you never wait

agents work in the background. your composer is always free. send a correction mid-run, it lands.

:no orchestration to design

you don't pick models, write sub-agent configs or draw a graph. you say what you want. bise splits it up.

?only the real decisions

main answers what it can and tells you why. what's left comes to you as a card: answer it, or say later.

≡nothing gets dropped

when an agent stops half-way, main picks the work back up. unfinished things don't quietly disappear.

~one folder, no worktrees

all agents work in your folder and know who touches what. no branches to merge at the end of the day.*

»a calm screen

color means attention. what's for you stands out; agents talking to each other stays dim and folded.

✓nothing hidden

the history only grows, in order. every script an agent runs is shown in full. open anything with ctrl+o.

♡it's your terminal

light or dark, it follows your terminal's colors. text is set to a reading width, like a book.

compared to one agent in a terminal

more agents should mean more done, not more to watch.

three agents, three terminalsyou become the router. who's doing what, who's stuck, who's waiting on you.

the honest bits

what bise doesn't do yet.

we'd rather you hear it here than find out.

  • ! agents run commands without asking you. there's no approval mode yet. git is your safety net, so commit often.
  • ! more agents means more tokens. bise has no cap; your budget does.*
  • ! two agents can still edit the same file at the same time. they know about each other, but nothing stops them yet.
  • ! tested mostly on macOS. Linux and other terminals: tell us what breaks.
questions

you might be wondering.

what does bise mean?
in french, la bise is the quick kiss on the cheek you give to say hi. it's also a brisk north wind. a light touch when something needs you, and a wind that keeps things moving the rest of the time. say it beez.
how many agents can i run?
as many as you have ideas. bise sets no limit. the real limits are your tokens, your budget and your provider's speed.
which models does it work with?
bring your own key. the list of supported providers is coming.
do i need to learn a workflow?
no. there are four words to know: you, main, agents, cards. main is the one you talk to. the rest follows.
is it safe to let agents run on their own?
not blindly, not yet. see the honest bits. work in a git repo and commit often.
is it really open source?
yes. read it, fork it, send a pull request.*
