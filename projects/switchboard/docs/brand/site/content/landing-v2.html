the reader

she uses Claude Code or Codex every day. two or three terminals open. she's happy with it. she has never heard of bise, and the page has 10 seconds to earn 60 more.

her questions, in order:

  1. what is it? (another agent? a wrapper? a model?)
  2. why would i switch? (what does it do that my tool doesn't?)
  3. does it work with my stuff? (models, MCP servers, plugins, repo, git)
  4. what do i lose, what's the risk?
  5. how do i try it in 2 minutes?

rule: the first screen answers 1 and 2. no feature list before she knows what it is.

one-sentence answer to "what is it": a terminal app where you talk to one agent, and it runs a whole team of agents for you.

the 4 ideas, as i understood them

each one gets a sentence and a proof (what we show so it's believed, not just said).

1
one thread per project. no tabs, no sessions to find again. you always talk to the same agent, main. main is your team lead, you're the boss. you don't micro-manage: you say your ideas, the work gets done.
proof: a whole day in one scroll. 14 things shipped, one conversation.
2
a team that runs itself. the agents know each other. they message each other before they collide. they make their own worktrees when they need one and clean up after, commit their own work, keep the history clean.
proof: two agents about to edit the same file sort it out in two messages. you never see it unless you look.
3
main is always there. main never goes quiet while the team works. ask a question, add a thing, change your mind: it answers right away. the composer is never locked.
proof: you type while 5 agents run, main answers in a second.
4
opinionated. it just works. you don't design a multi-agent workflow. no roles to write, no graph, no config. you can forget it's multi-agent at all: the complexity disappears.
proof: zero setup screen. install, type one sentence, 3 agents are working.

the outcome, not a 5th idea: you stay in flow. all four serve it. that's why "flow" goes in the hero and never in a feature title.

question: you said main "has the context of all your projects". today it's one workspace per folder. do you mean all the work in one project, or really several projects? i wrote "per project" for now.
