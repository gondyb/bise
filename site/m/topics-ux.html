N jobs in one thread

Benjamin: "a chat is linear, and your team lead juggles N topics in parallel… hard to keep the thread." you: "for now i like the linear side: i can scroll back in time. it's fine if it isn't too busy."

so the question: how do you see and act on N jobs without losing the timeline? five directions, the same hour in each, drawn in real terminal cells. my pick: A, the thread with a job lens.

the story

the same hour in every mock. at 14:05 you ask for four things in one message. main starts 4 agents: dark-mode, csv-fix, tests, notes. the jobs are not the agents: tests fixes the flaky login, then writes the test for the emoji csv crash. 14:42 (the first tab everywhere): flaky login and emoji csv are done, release notes are being written, dark-mode asks you which gray? 15:12 ("30 min later"): you answered, the notes are done, and you scroll back to see what happened to the csv crash.

before: today

one thread, the agents on the right, the inbox above the composer. four jobs live in the thread as blocks between other blocks: to follow the csv crash, you read all 8 blocks and keep 3.

A · the thread, with a job lensmy pick

every block in main's thread belongs to a job; main sets it, like it already sets an agent's role line. at rest the only new thing is the job's name, faint, on the right of a block when the job changes (a chapter mark, not a label on every line). ctrl+t opens the job list in the inbox's place; ⏎ narrows the thread to that job, Zulip style: the other blocks fold into one faint ┄ 3 on other jobs ┄ line each, so you still see that time passed. you answer in place, and esc gives you the whole thread back at the same spot.

+ gains
  • the timeline stays the base: scroll back works as today, and narrowing is a filter on it, never another place
  • one key in, esc out. reading the csv story at 15:12 is 4 blocks instead of 12
  • at rest it adds one faint word per job change, and nothing at 80 cols that 100 doesn't have
− costs
  • main has to tag every block with a job; a wrong tag hides a block from the narrowed view (the fold line says how many, so it's never silent)
  • no new word: the landing already says main splits your ideas into jobs; you keep you, main, agents, inbox
  • ctrl+t was expand until it was retired; some old muscle memory may remain

keys new: ctrl+t jobs (free since the old expand key was removed), ↑↓ ⏎ in the list, esc out. kept: ctrl+1 answers, ⌥0-9 agents, ctrl+o, ctrl+f (find works inside the narrowed view).

B · a job board

the panel lists jobs instead of agents at rest: four rows, a glyph each. the agents come back under their job when you hold ctrl (tests sits under two). ctrl+t opens the board in the thread's place, Gmail style: one row per job with its last line and its question. ⏎ opens the narrowed thread from A; esc goes back to the board, then to the thread.

+ gains
  • the panel answers "what's going on" in 4 rows, calmer than 5 agents + an inbox section
  • the board gives every job its question and last line on one screen
  • agent on two jobs reads naturally under ctrl
− costs
  • two places to look (board, thread), and a mode to remember
  • agents leave the panel at rest: ⌥0-9 lose their visible numbers until you hold ctrl
  • under 90 cols there is no panel: the board is the only way to see jobs

keys new: ctrl+t the board, ↑↓ ⏎ esc inside. the panel's agent rows show their ⌥ number on ctrl only.

C · lanes

the thread as a git graph. one lane per job, a node per event, one row per event; a done job joins the trunk. the legend sits on the split row, in lane order. the surprise: it is shorter than today (one row per event instead of a block), and your messages and main's stay on the trunk.

+ gains
  • the most on brand: the landing's hero already draws agents as branches
  • you see at a glance when jobs ran together and when one ended
  • one row per event: denser than today
− costs
  • busy: with 8 jobs the gutter is 18 cells of lines before a word, and crossings (┼) at every merge
  • scrolled back, the lanes come from off screen: the legend has to be pinned to stay readable
  • a lane per job means a job can't pause and come back without a long empty lane

keys no new key at rest. narrowing to one lane would reuse A's ctrl+t.

D · status above, log below

one line pinned at the top of the thread: every job and its glyph, which main edits in place. hold ctrl and it opens into one row per job with its last line. scrolled back, the line shows the state at the time you're reading, not now: at 14:24 three jobs were still running.

+ gains
  • the "what's going on" answer is always on screen, the log stays exactly as today
  • the time-travel line makes scrolling back easier to read: you see the state then
  • nothing to learn: no new key
− costs
  • the question shows twice (the pinned line and the inbox), at both ends of the screen
  • past 5 jobs the line is cut, and a one-line summary can't say much
  • it's a summary, not a way to act: you still dig through the log to follow one job

keys nothing new; ctrl held opens the block (ctrl already means details).

E · cards, no chat (bold)

no thread on screen. one card per job, its state, its last lines and its question with the answers right in it. the composer talks to the selected card; esc and you talk to main, which makes or picks the card. ⏎ opens a card's own thread. what you lose shows at 15:12: done cards shrink to a line, and the order between jobs is gone.

+ gains
  • the most visual: four jobs, four boxes, the question answered where it's asked
  • the composer is aimed: no "which one do you mean?"
  • what Benjamin asked for: the chat is still under the hood
− costs
  • the scroll back in time is gone: "what happened at 14:30" has no screen
  • card boxes take 6 rows each: 4 jobs fill 100×40, a 5th scrolls
  • main's own voice ("on it", "i answered notes") has no place; you lose the sense of a teammate

keys ctrl+t next card, ⏎ its thread, esc main, 1-3 answer. no clash, but tab-like cycling in a text box is easy to miss.

the pick: A, with B's list as its door

A keeps what you said you like, the scroll back in time, and adds one faint word per job change at rest. the job list it opens on ctrl+t is B's board made small, in the inbox's place, so there is one new key and no new screen. from D i'd keep one idea for later: when you scroll back, the header could say the state at that time. C is the landing's picture, and it stays there: at 8 jobs it's a wall of lines. E shows what Benjamin meant, and what it costs: the timeline.

how it behaves

  • tags: main tags every block it writes about one job; a block about several jobs (or none) stays untagged and shows in every narrowed view.
  • end: a job ends when main reports it done; typing in a narrowed view reopens it.
  • ctrl+t, narrowed: opens the list with the current job selected; esc goes back to everything.
