01 · philosophy

one thread. a whole team. you, in flow.

agents made us fast. they also made our days feel empty.

you start five things in five tabs, and each one waits for you: "should i continue?", "which gray?", "v1 or v2?". you're the router, the memory and the merge tool. at 6pm a lot got merged, and you feel like you did nothing. you gave away the fun part, and got nothing back.

it's not you. it's the switching. forty times a day something pulls you out of your head, and flow, the state where you think clearly and the hours disappear, never gets a chance to start.

so bise gives you one thread.

one thread per repo, for good: compaction keeps the context, you never start over. you talk to main, bise's main agent. main splits your ideas into jobs, starts agents in the background, answers the obvious questions the way you would, and taps your shoulder only when a decision is really yours. and main is always listening: say the next thing while the last one runs.

behind it, the team runs itself. agents message each other before they collide, make a worktree only when one helps and clean it up after, and commit their own work. you don't design a workflow: bise has opinions, so you don't need any. after a day you forget there are agents at all.

built for humans piloting hundreds of robots, from a distance.

a bise is a little kiss, and a cold north wind. that's the whole idea: a light touch on the shoulder when something needs you, and the rest of the time, a breeze that keeps everything moving while you think.

what we believe

  • flow is the product. everything else is plumbing.
  • one conversation beats ten tabs. you talk; the orchestration is our job.
  • the human has the ideas. the tech keeps up, then gets out of the way.
  • more agents should mean more done, never more to watch.
  • an agent starts when a job needs one. that's the whole rule: no committee of agents checking each other, no tokens burned for show.
  • a calm screen is a feature. what's for you stands out; the rest is folded, one key away.
  • trust comes from honesty: say what happened, say what isn't built, never pretend.

02 · values

five things we never trade away.

∿flow

you never wait on a turn. say it, keep talking. the work runs in the background and bise is always listening.

›control

you decide. bise answers the obvious questions itself, shows why, and brings you only the real decisions.

:*lightness

no project management, no workflow to design, four words to learn. worktrees and conflicts are handled for you. many hands, light work.

♡calm

color means attention. agents talk to each other quietly; what's for you stands out, the rest stays dim.

✓honesty

no silent failure, no hidden limit, no feature we pretend to have. the history never lies: it only grows, in order.

03 · name and mark

bise :*

the name is always lowercase, like a command you type: bise. four letters, easy to type a hundred times a day.

the mark is :*, the kiss you type on a keyboard. it works in any terminal and any font, and looks typed by a human. inside the product it marks main's voice.

say it: beez. in english the word is rare, so we explain it wherever a stranger meets it first (site hero, README, launch posts), with a dictionary entry: bise /beez/ · french, n. 1. a quick kiss on the cheek. 2. a brisk north wind. 3. a terminal where multi-agent coding is painless. short form, one line: bise (say: beez): french for a kiss. also a wind. not inside the product: there, the :* says it.

standalone. bise is its own brand: no borrowed colors, no parent logo.

04 · voice and tone

a human, typing fast.

lowercase, always

no capital letter at the start of a word, anywhere: site, UI, messages. it reads like someone typing, not a company talking.

  • proper nouns keep their capitals: GitHub, Claude Code, Ghostty.
  • acronyms stay in caps: API, MCP, PR. "cli" in lowercase is fine.
  • ALL CAPS to shout: allowed, extremely rarely.
  • our name stays lowercase: bise.

no period at the end of a title

a stack of titles that all end with a period sounds like a list of verdicts. so titles drop their last period: sections, features, lists, UI labels. all your MCPs. all your skills. always on: the periods in the middle stay, only the last one goes. the numbered meanings of the gloss (1. a quick kiss on the cheek) drop it too.

  • keep it only on the headline and on real punchlines, where the period is the timing of the joke: kiss your backlog goodbye., one thread. a whole team., no yaml was harmed. one per screen, at most.
  • body text keeps its periods.

who speaks

inside the product, bise says "i", and "you" is always the user. on the site, we talk to the reader as "you".

four words, no more

the user meets you, main, agents, cards. never "task", "sub-agent", "orchestrator", "hub" or "hands" in the UI.

do / don't

do

say it and keep talking. the work runs in the background, i'm always here.

docs asked v1 or v2; the brief says v2, so i answered.

glm-5 can't read images. pick a model that can, or describe the screen in words.

don't

Welcome! Unleash the power of seamless multi-agent orchestration.

Task #3 (docs) has been resolved by the Orchestrator.

Error 400: invalid_request_error: image_url not supported.

  • short sentences. show the moment, don't name the feeling.
  • warm, a bit cheeky, never cute at the expense of clear.
  • no promise with a number. the real limits are tokens, cost and provider speed: say so.
  • no polished LLM patterns: "X. none of the Y.", "seamless", "supercharge", "unleash".

05 · visual identity

terminal brut, with the soul of a bise.

raw terminal credibility: monospace, calm, the product is the visual. plus one warm human touch: the pale pink accent, the :*, a heart for "done".

the background is yours

in the terminal we never paint the background: your terminal's color, transparency and blur show through. we only choose the colors of the text. two themes, light and dark, picked from your terminal's background at start. try the switch in the menu: this whole page follows it.

palette · dark

  • color means attention. only "needs you" and errors get a hue. the accent is pink, not red, so "needs you" never looks like an error.
  • every text you read is at least 4.5:1 on white, cream, black and a typical dark grey. faint is for rails and borders only.

type

JetBrains Mono for the site and screenshots. in the terminal, the font is yours.

motion

only four things move: the working breeze ∿, the cursor , the typed welcome, and the :* pop. nothing bounces, nothing slides.

07 · ux principles

seven rules for every screen.

1you stay in control, the screen stays calm. agents work quietly; only what needs you gets color.
2everything is readable. prose never wider than a novel line; contrast ≥ 4.5:1.
3progressive disclosure. one line per step, details one key away. scripts are the exception: always in full.
4one symbol per thing.
5the history never lies. it only grows, in arrival order.
6no new vocabulary. you, main, agents, cards.
7honest. no silent failure, no hidden limit, nothing we pretend to have.

10 · the history

it only grows. in order.

the history is append-only, in arrival order. nothing is grouped by agent, nothing moves later. what changes over time (status, age, open cards) lives outside it: the panel, the header, the card box.

  • content is frozen; small marks are not. the only change on an existing line is its status mark, like ✓ becoming ✓✓.
  • only the tail can grow. more than 3 messages between agents fold into one dim line. only the last run, at the bottom, still grows; a line for you closes it forever.
  • time marks after a pause: a faint · 14:31 · after 5 minutes of silence.

13 · talking to agents

just talk. that's the whole interface.

to main

say what you want, as many things as you want, one after the other. main says who takes what.

to one agent

@name sends a direct message without leaving main. ⌥ + number or ⏎ on an agent takes you inside it; esc brings you back.

did it get my message?

while an agent works, what you send waits for its turn. the marks at the end of your line tell you where it is, like a text message.
